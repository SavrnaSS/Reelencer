# Gig reference pictures

In the gig editor, use **Media → Reference pictures & files · after approval** to upload the pictures creators should include in their reels. Save the gig to publish the changes. Existing `Media::` uploads continue to work without a migration; public `Sample::` reel examples remain separate.

After an application is accepted, its approval panel shows the reference gallery before the next steps. Content Posting gigs explain that creators should show the pictures in their reel as described in the brief; pending and rejected Content Posting applications do not render the gallery. Other gig types use general reference-file guidance. Email Creator gigs retain file access through an active assignment. Gigs without references do not render an empty section.

The gallery fits its available column width and displays entire pictures without cropping; a single reference gets the full width. Each card shows the picture's orientation, ratio and pixel size, and a Download button (Supabase uploads download as attachments under their original name; other hosts open the original in a new tab). With several Supabase uploads, **Download all** saves them in one go.

Content Posting galleries add an **Original / In a reel** switch. The reel view places each picture in a 9:16 phone frame over a blurred copy of itself, shades the areas reel captions and buttons usually cover, and gives a fit tip (for example, landscape screenshots are small in a vertical reel, so creators should zoom in or pan across).

Admins can add an **in-reel example** to any reference picture in the gig editor (Media → each uploaded picture → *Add in-reel example*): a 9:16 screenshot or short clip showing exactly how the picture should appear in the reel. It is saved as a `Media::Reel::{"for":pictureUrl,"url":exampleUrl}` line, so it stays private like other `Media::` lines and older readers ignore it. Removing a picture also removes its example. When a gig has examples, the creator gallery opens in the **In a reel** view and shows the admin's example (labelled *Example*) instead of the automatic preview; pictures without one keep the automatic frame. A single reference is shown side by side with its guidance, the original picture and a Download button, and stacks on phones.

Pictures and videos open in a full-screen viewer with previous/next controls, a thumbnail strip, arrow keys and horizontal swipes. Tapping a picture zooms it to full size for reading small details. Content Posting pictures can also be previewed in a reel frame there, with Fit or Fill screen framing to show what a full-screen crop would cut. Escape closes the viewer and restores focus to the preview. Download/original links stay available if a preview fails. Video cards fetch only metadata for a preview frame; the clip itself loads when opened.

The normal dashboard refresh picks up approval decisions and saved gig media changes. Removing the current reference or revoking approval closes its viewer. This feature retains the existing upload bucket and URL access behavior; the approval condition controls where the gallery appears in the dashboard.

## Explaining how to use each picture

Each uploaded picture in the gig editor has a **What should creators show?** note (up to 240 characters), saved as a private `Media::Note::{"for":pictureUrl,"text":…}` line and removed with its picture.

For Content Posting gigs, creators see under a single picture (or in a collapsible **How to use these pictures** panel above several):

- **What to show** – the admin's note, highlighted. It also appears on the picture's card, in the reel view and in the full-screen viewer.
- **Ways to feature it in your reel** – three options with a one-tap action, worded for the picture's shape: *Film it on a screen* (opens it full screen, or jumps to the reel kit's demo wallet when the gig has one), *Add it in your editor* (download, with zoom-and-pan advice for wide pictures) and *Talk in front of it* (green screen, with a reel-frame preview).
- **Before you post** – a four-item checklist that shows "Ready to post" when complete. Ticks are remembered in that browser only.

The guide lays out by its own width (three columns when there is room, stacked on phones).

## Reel kit (Content Posting)

For reels that show the creator wallet, turn on **Reel kit · demo wallet screen** under Media in the gig editor. Set the sample figures (approved and pending earnings with their reel counts, paid out, pending withdrawal), a **hook caption idea** (for the opening selfie) and a **closing caption idea** (for the wallet clip) – creators write their own captions; these are shown as ideas, optional notes for creators (one per line) and an optional example reel (the sample creators should copy). **Preview demo wallet** opens what creators will film. The kit is saved as a private `Media::Kit::{json}` line; figures are clamped and text trimmed when read.

Approved creators see a compact **Record your reel** panel above the reference pictures. It sits flat inside the gig card (no border of its own); only its header has a soft tinted background.

- **Header** – **Record your reel**, two short sentences (follow the sample: a selfie hook, quick clips of Reelencer, then a demo wallet ready to film; shoot it on your phone, add your own captions and post), a progress ring with *4 quick steps* / *n of 4 steps done* / *All steps done*, and an **Open demo wallet** button.
- **How to make it** – four steps shown as an open checklist (numbered dots joined by a thin line that turns green as steps finish, no boxes) whose title and summary are always visible; the current step opens in place and **Done, next step** ticks it off and opens the next; a finished step shows *Done · Undo*. Progress is remembered per gig in that browser, and the admin's notes appear above the steps as **Notes from {company}**.
  1. *Set up* – the reel is always filmed with the creator's phone camera. A **Laptop | Second phone** switch picks the device that shows the screen. A compact vector illustration (`RecordingSetupArtwork`) shows a phone filming that device; the camera phone’s viewfinder shows the same wallet on the laptop or second phone. They include a laptop keyboard or phone stand, a hand holding the camera phone, portrait framing and a recording indicator. The illustration and three titled tips (bright and full screen, no glare, phone upright and slightly angled – `SETUP_STEPS` in `lib/reelKit.ts`) stack in narrow containers and sit side by side when there is room, followed by a one-line *You'll need* list. Reduced motion disables the recording-light and camera-direction animation.
  2. *Open the demo wallet* – open, copy or send the link to that device; keyboard or touch controls.
  3. *Film the clips* – the example reel as seven 9:16 frames modelled on the gig's sample (about 14 s): **Your hook** (front camera), then back-camera clips filmed off the screen – **Chrome**, **Search** (reelencer.com), **Home page**, **Find gigs**, **Proposal** – and **Wallet** (the demo wallet's count-up). Step 3 is one stage that never shows a scene twice. On phones and in narrow columns, the seven clips are a row of small numbered frames (a blank white bar in each marks where its caption goes), with a phone preview beside the selected clip's directions below. In wider containers, the preview, the directions and a compact clip list (tiny frame, number, title, length, progress line) sit side by side. The directions show *Clip n of 7*, the title, ‹ › arrows, camera and length chips, one paragraph of filming direction and a **Caption idea** (creators write their own wording); the wallet clip adds **Open demo wallet**. **Play preview** plays the suggested 14-second edit with scene motion; thin bars under the frames show its progress. Arrow keys, Home and End navigate the clips; reduced-motion preferences disable scene animation. The list lives in `reelShots()` in `lib/reelKit.ts`; a link to the sample reel appears when one was uploaded.
  4. *Edit and post* – put the clips in the example's order (about 14 s), write a short relatable caption for each in your own words (white box, bold black text; the hook mid-screen, the rest lower middle), cut on the beat of an upbeat sound, export 9:16, post and submit the link from Linked.

It lays out by its own width, so it reads the same in the approval column, full width and on phones.

The demo wallet (`/reel-kit/wallet`) renders the real creator wallet view inside a copy of the dashboard shell, filled with the gig's figures and the creator's first name. Figures travel in the URL, so it needs no sign-in and shows no account data; the sidebar brand line (laptop layouts) reads **Demo workspace**. **Play take** (Space) hides the controls and cursor at once and keeps them hidden for the whole take: an optional silent 3-second countdown (the screen holds at ₹0), the figures counting up while the Reel earnings card glows, then the screen is browsed like a person would – a pointer (a fingertip dot on touch screens) rests on Approved earnings, the page scrolls down to the payout cycles in a few trackpad-style flicks with a slight overshoot and correction, and the pointer opens the latest payout cycle to show its line item before the hold. Distances are measured on that screen. S turns the browsing off. Mid-take, single taps and mouse movement are ignored; Esc or R (or, on touch screens, three quick taps) stops the take and brings the controls back. Scrolling the page by hand hides the controls; a tap or mouse move brings them back. On phones the controls become a full-width Play take button above labelled tiles (Timer, Browse, Reset, Name, Full, Hide) with touch-specific instructions; larger screens show one row with keyboard hints. R resets, F toggles full screen, H hides the controls. The screen stays awake during takes where the browser supports it, and reduced-motion settings skip the count-up and jump straight to the payouts. The page is excluded from search engines.
